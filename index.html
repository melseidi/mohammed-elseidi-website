01 — About

Statistician, forecaster, and quality-assurance leader.

I build forecasting models that pair classical time-series decomposition with modern machine learning, and I apply them to environmental and social data — wind speed, temperature and national well-being — where seasonality is complex and the stakes are real.

My work is published in top-decile (Q1) Statistics journals across Scopus and Web of Science, with a Field-Weighted Citation Impact of 1.84 — cited 84% more than the world average. Alongside research I direct quality assurance and institutional effectiveness at Umm Al Quwain University, serve as an expert with the IUCN Commission on Environmental, Economic and Social Policy, and edit for Taylor & Francis, the Royal Meteorological Society and Frontiers.

03 — Forecast Lab

Run my published models on your own data.

Both hybrid frameworks are implemented end to end in JavaScript and run entirely inside your browser. Drop in a CSV, choose a model, and the decomposition and the neural network are fitted on your own machine — free, and without an account.

  • Nothing is uploaded. Your data never leaves your device — there is no server to send it to.
  • Full output. Point forecasts with prediction intervals, seasonal-trend decomposition, residual diagnostics and a downloadable CSV.
  • Honest evaluation. Every run is scored on a hold-out sample against a seasonal-naive benchmark.
  • Built for teaching. Automatic seasonality detection and example series make it a ready-made classroom demonstration.
Open the Forecast Lab CSV, TSV or Excel · up to ~50,000 rows

MSTL-NNAR

Model 01

Multiple seasonal-trend decomposition using LOESS, followed by a neural-network autoregression on the seasonally adjusted series — built for data with more than one seasonal cycle.

Elseidi, M. (2024). Stochastic Environmental Research and Risk Assessment, 38(7), 2613–2632.

STR-NBEATS

Model 02

Seasonal-trend decomposition by regression, which lets the seasonal shape evolve, combined with an N-BEATS deep stack that forecasts every step of the horizon at once.

Elseidi, M. (2025). Modeling Earth Systems and Environment, 11(4), 255.
