Method

How a run works

1 · Read and inspect

The file is parsed in the page — delimiter, header row, date format and decimal convention are all detected from the data itself. Gaps are filled by linear interpolation before fitting, and the number filled is reported.

2 · Find the seasonality

Candidate periods come from the detected sampling frequency (24 and 168 for hourly data, 7 and 365 for daily, and so on). Each is scored by the seasonal-strength measure of Wang, Smith & Hyndman, and accepted one at a time on the strength that remains once the cycles already chosen are removed. That is what stops a pure 12-month cycle from also being modelled at 24 months. The bar rises with the length of the period, because a long cycle fitted to a short series explains a good share of the variance by chance alone.

3 · Decompose

MSTL applies STL repeatedly, peeling off the shortest cycle first and iterating so the components settle. STR instead solves one penalised least-squares problem for the trend and every seasonal component at once, which lets the seasonal shape drift slowly over the sample; it is solved matrix-free with preconditioned conjugate gradients.

4 · Model what is left

The seasonal parts are extrapolated forward and the seasonally adjusted series is handed to the learner. NNAR is a single-hidden-layer network on lagged values, trained from several random starts and averaged, forecasting one step at a time and feeding its own output back. N-BEATS is a stack of fully connected blocks — each removes a backcast from the residual and adds a forecast to the total — with polynomial trend and Fourier seasonality bases, producing the whole horizon in one shot.

5 · Score and report

With a hold-out set, the model is fitted on the earlier part only, asked for the hold-out window, and scored against a seasonal-naive benchmark. It is then refitted on the whole series for the forecast you take away. MSTL-NNAR intervals come from bootstrapping residuals through the recursive path; STR-NBEATS uses the empirical spread of its validation errors at each step of the horizon.

What this is, and is not

These are faithful browser re-implementations of the two published frameworks, written for exploration and teaching. They use fixed sensible defaults rather than the hyper-parameter searches behind the papers, and a compact N-BEATS rather than the full ensemble, so they will not reproduce the published figures exactly. Treat the output as a well-founded first pass, not as a replacement for a fitted study.
